# Roteiro: ep01-ai-bubble

[narrador] On Monday at 9:31 AM, Nvidia lost a trillion dollars.

[narrador] By Friday, Dan Rivera's biggest client had cancelled.

[narrador] This part is a simulation. The numbers behind it are real.

[narrador] This is what the next thirty days could look like for one ordinary family.

[narrador] Meet the Riveras. Sunday night, the day before it all starts.

[narrador] Dan is forty-two, a self-employed electrician, and possibly the most optimistic man in Ohio.

[narrador] Mia is a nurse. She reads the fine print, and she's usually right.

[narrador] Leo is sixteen. He has twenty-three hundred dollars in AI stocks, from one summer of mowing lawns.

[narrador] Bea is eight. Biscuit is a dog, and he believes the entire house belongs to him.

[narrador] Here is what they have. Six thousand two hundred forty dollars in the bank. Two weeks of food. Bills paid. Everyone healthy. Keep your eyes on this panel.

[narrador] Money in: Mia brings home forty-three hundred a month. Dan averages sixty-two hundred. Money out: seventy-nine hundred. It works. Barely.

[narrador] But sixty percent of Dan's income comes from one client: a contractor building a giant AI data center on the edge of town.

[Dan] Best job I ever had. They can't build these things fast enough.

[narrador] He is right. Until Monday.

## DAY 1: MONDAY

[narrador] Monday, 9:31 AM. One minute after the opening bell, sell orders flood in.

[narrador] In this simulation, Nvidia falls twenty-one percent by lunch. Close to a trillion dollars, gone.

[narrador] That sounds impossible, until you remember the real record. In January 2025, Nvidia lost five hundred eighty-nine billion dollars in one day.

[narrador] So why would it pop? Picture a circle. A chip maker invests in an AI lab. The lab rents computing power. That money comes back around to buy more chips.

[narrador] It works as long as real customers eventually pay enough to justify it. Critics have warned about these circular deals for more than a year.

[narrador] In our scenario, three of the biggest buyers quietly cut their spending plans on the same morning. The circle stops.

[narrador] At school, Leo's phone buzzes in third period. His portfolio is already down thirty-one percent.

[narrador] Mia is on the floor at the hospital. She won't hear about it until lunch.

[narrador] Dan hears it on the truck radio and turns it down. Stocks are for people in suits, he says. He's got wire to pull.

[narrador] Here is what he misses. The S&P 500 sits inside millions of retirement accounts, and its ten biggest companies make up nearly forty percent of it.

[narrador] Dan keeps sixty-eight thousand dollars in a retirement account. He thought it was boring and safe. By dinner, it is down eleven thousand.

## DAY 3: WEDNESDAY

[narrador] Wednesday. The mortgage comes out, like it does every month. Twenty-three forty.

[narrador] On TV, the word is correction.

[narrador] It might be. But when the last tech bubble burst, the Nasdaq fell seventy-eight percent, and it took about fifteen years to come back.

[narrador] At the site, nobody is pouring concrete. The crew is told to go home and wait for a phone call.

[Dan] It's a pause. Big jobs pause.

## DAY 5: FRIDAY

[narrador] Friday afternoon. Mia's paycheck lands. For a moment, the panel looks fine.

[narrador] Seven minutes later, Dan's phone rings. It's the contractor. The project isn't paused. It's cancelled.

[narrador] Not delayed. Not downsized. Cancelled.

[narrador] They still owe him twenty-three thousand four hundred dollars for finished work. Sixty-day terms. Their lawyer tells him to get in line.

[narrador] That was sixty percent of his income. And he is still holding ninety-eight hundred dollars of copper wire, bought on his credit card for a job that no longer exists.

## DAY 7: SUNDAY

[narrador] Sunday night, at the kitchen table. The truck payment clears. Mia opens a spreadsheet.

[narrador] Without Dan's work, money in is forty-three hundred. Money out is still seventy-nine hundred. They are burning thirty-six hundred dollars a month.

[narrador] At that rate, the account hits zero in about forty days. And that is if nothing else goes wrong.

[Bea] Are we poor now?

[Mia] No, sweetie. We're just being careful.

[narrador] Then Mia goes to the laptop and cancels every monthly charge she can find. Streaming. The gym. All of it. Remember that. It matters later.

[narrador] They are not unusual. In the Federal Reserve's own survey, only sixty-three percent of American adults said they could cover a four-hundred-dollar emergency with cash.

## DAY 9: TUESDAY

[narrador] Here is the part most people miss. A bubble does not pop in one place. It travels.

[narrador] Data center crews go first.

[narrador] Then steel suppliers.

[narrador] Truck drivers.

[narrador] The diner next to the site.

[narrador] By now the diner has cut its hours. Dan's electrician friends are calling him, asking if he has heard of any work.

[narrador] He posts an ad online and gets eleven calls. Nine of them ask him to match the price of a guy who was laid off last week.

## DAY 12: FRIDAY

[narrador] An email from the bank. His credit limit just dropped from eighteen thousand to eleven thousand. He has never missed a payment.

[narrador] That part is not fiction. FICO estimated that thirty-three million Americans had their available credit cut in just six months of the 2008 crisis. Banks shrink your safety net exactly when you need it.

## DAY 14: THE CHOICE

[narrador] Thirty-seven hundred dollars left. The next mortgage is two weeks away. Dan and Mia have two options.

[narrador] Option A. Dan pulls ten thousand dollars out of his retirement account. He's forty-two, so that's a ten percent penalty plus income tax. He keeps about seventy-eight hundred, and sells near the bottom.

[narrador] Option B. Mia calls the bank and asks for forbearance: pause the mortgage for three months. The debt doesn't vanish, and it means admitting out loud that they can't pay.

[narrador] What would you do? Comment A or B, right now, before you see what happens.

[Dan] We're not calling the bank. Riveras pay their bills.

[narrador] Mia says nothing. That is worse.

[narrador] Dan picks A. The transfer clears Monday morning. The panel jumps, and for one day it feels like winning.

[narrador] The next day, the market bounces nine percent. Dan sold within a day of the bottom, and it cost him the most expensive money he will ever spend.

## DAY 17: WEDNESDAY

[narrador] Because it is never just one thing, Biscuit eats a full box of Bea's colored pencils.

[narrador] Emergency vet. Sixteen hundred forty dollars. He is going to be fine. The panel is not.

[narrador] Mia picks up every extra shift she can get. Four twelve-hour days in a row. Her paycheck is now the whole family's income.

[narrador] At the store, the cart changes. Store brand. Less meat. Rice and beans. Nobody says anything about it. Everybody notices.

## DAY 20: SATURDAY

[narrador] Leo finally opens the app he has been avoiding. Twenty-three hundred dollars is now two hundred ninety-nine. Down eighty-seven percent.

[narrador] He sells what's left, walks downstairs, and puts it on the table. Nobody asked him to. Dan cannot look at him.

[narrador] The next morning, Dan sells the copper back at a loss and pays off the rest of the card, before twenty-five percent interest eats him alive. Sixty-four hundred dollars.

## DAY 24: WEDNESDAY

[narrador] A pink envelope arrives. Shut-off notice. When Mia cancelled everything, she also cancelled the electric company's autopay.

[narrador] That night, for the first time in this story, they fight. Not really about the bill. About the phone call he would not let her make.

[narrador] Bea hears all of it through her bedroom wall. Biscuit, stitches and all, walks over and sits beside her.

[narrador] Look at the panel. Stress at ninety-four. Health, low. They still have forty-eight hundred dollars. This is the real cost, and no chart on TV ever shows it.

## DAY 27: SATURDAY

[narrador] Saturday morning, Dan pays the electric bill. Then he does the thing he swore he would not do. He calls the bank.

[narrador] It takes forty minutes. Nothing terrible happens. The pause starts next month.

[narrador] Then Mia hears something at work. The hospital's backup generators just failed an inspection, and nobody can get an electrician for a week.

[narrador] It is small work.

[narrador] Then a neighbor's panel.

[narrador] Then the school.

[narrador] People without money fix what they already own, and that takes an electrician.

[narrador] Not sixty percent from one client. Three small ones in four days. Nineteen hundred dollars. It is the first time in a month that Dan smiles.

## DAY 30: THE VERDICT

[narrador] Day thirty. The last mortgage payment before the pause comes out. They are still in the house, and the lights are still on.

[narrador] Here's the score. They kept the house, and both of them are working. But the unpaid invoice is still twenty-three thousand. The retirement account took a hit it may need years to recover from. And Leo's summer is gone. Sixty-one out of one hundred. They survived. They are not okay.

[narrador] And thirty days is only the start. After the 2008 crisis, American unemployment passed ten percent, more than a year and a half after the recession began.

[narrador] So here are three things you can do today. One: keep one month of expenses in cash, before you need it. Two: never let one client, one employer, or one industry pay most of your bills. Three: look up what your retirement fund really owns. If it tracks the S&P 500, nearly forty percent sits in ten companies.

[narrador] Did you pick A or B? Tell us in the comments. Next month, you decide what the Riveras do.

[narrador] Next week. The grid goes down for fourteen days. Hour one feels like a camping trip. By hour seventy-two, the water stops.
